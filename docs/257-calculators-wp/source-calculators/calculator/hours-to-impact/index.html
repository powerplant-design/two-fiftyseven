Where it goes

Charities, climate organisations, and tangata whenua-led work.

When a team books a desk at full rate, part of that rent funds another desk for an organisation that couldn't pay full rate. When a company hires a meeting room or a workshop space, that booking funds time for another organisation. When an event takes over the venue, the same redistribution happens at scale.

We're not running an office that has a charity arm. We're running a property model that redistributes by design. The Impact Discount is a 50% rate (or more · talk to us) for organisations doing work this kaupapa supports.

At scale

Imagine if the whole commercial property industry worked this way.

two/fiftyseven exists for two reasons · one is sustainability and one is community, and we really think those things are the same thing. The model we run here · paying members enabling subsidised space for the organisations doing the work · is small, in one place, on one corner of one city. The maths it implies at scale is the real argument.

More than a third of global emissions come from buildings. In a world where billion-dollar climate disasters now hit every twelve days, and New Zealand's storm-related insurance claims have tripled in the last year, we can't afford to keep building the way we do.

If every commercial building in Aotearoa ran a redistribution model proportional to ours, the redistribution into climate work, tangata whenua-led work, and community organisations would land in the billions of dollars annually · closer to $5 billion than to $5 million. That's not a small adjustment to the existing system. That's the system, doing its actual job.

We're a working prototype. Five years in. $450,000 redistributed so far. The model works at this size. The question is whether it scales · and the only way to answer that is to keep proving it can.

How the math works

How we verify the $1-per-hour ratio.

The ratio is conservative. Across five years of operating two/fiftyseven, the actual redistribution has consistently outpaced what $1 per person-hour would imply. Reality runs ahead of the figure · which is the point, but also worth being clear about.

  1. The mechanism

    Every person-hour at two/fiftyseven by a paying member contributes approximately $1 to subsidised space · meeting rooms, day passes, memberships, event venues · for organisations on the Impact Discount.

  2. How the ratio is set

    Derived from five years of measured revenue versus measured discount given. The $1/hour figure sits below the actual realised ratio in each of those years · so the calculator under-promises by design.

  3. What counts as a discount

    The 50% Impact Discount on coworking memberships, meeting rooms, and venue hire. Plus negotiated rates that go further when a kaupapa needs it. Plus venue-hire fees waived entirely for events serving organisations we partner with.

  4. Annual review

    We re-check the ratio at the end of each financial year against measured giving. If the ratio drifts above $1/hour we'd consider revising the calculator up. Five years in it hasn't drifted down · and given the kaupapa, it shouldn't.
